Amrit: the Phoenician city south of Tartus

Ancient Marathus, about 6 km down the coast: what there is to see and how to fit it into a stay on the Corniche.

Where Amrit is

Amrit, the classical Marathus, lies on the Mediterranean coast about 6 km south of Tartus. It was a Phoenician port closely linked to the island of Arwad, just offshore. From the hotel it is a short drive along the coast. See the hotel's location.

The temple, the Ma'abed

The best-known monument is the temple called the Ma'abed: a large court cut into the rock, about 47 by 49 metres and more than 3 metres deep, with a stone shrine at its centre. It was dedicated to the god Melqart and dates from around the 4th century BC. It is often described as the best-preserved monumental building of the Phoenician homeland.

The stadium and the tombs

North of the temple are the remains of an ancient stadium, and to the south lies a necropolis with tomb towers and underground tombs. The city was largely abandoned in the 2nd century BC, which is why its ruins were never built over.

Planning the visit

Allow a morning or an afternoon, wear comfortable shoes and take water and a hat in summer; the site is open ground with little shade. Combine it with a walk on the Corniche at sunset. Opening days change, so ask at reception before you go. More things to do in Tartus.

Quick answers

How far is Amrit from Tartus?

About 6 km south of the city, a short drive along the coast from the Corniche.

What is there to see at Amrit?

The rock-cut Phoenician temple (the Ma'abed), the remains of an ancient stadium and a necropolis with tomb towers.

Where can I stay near Amrit?

Royal Inn is on the Tartus Sea Corniche, a short drive north of Amrit, with sea-view rooms from $90.
